### Data Ownership Roles
1. The data owner is the hospital — specifically, say, the head of the medical records department. They decide how sensitive the records are and who’s allowed to see them. In international-law language, the hospital is also the data controller.

2. The data custodian is the person doing the daily upkeep — for example, the hospital’s database administrator who actually configures the access permissions, runs the backups, and applies the encryption settings. But notice: they’re only doing what the owner directed. They don’t decide the rules; they enforce them.

3. The data processor is the cloud provider — say, the company running the servers where those records physically sit. They store and move the data on the hospital’s behalf, but they don’t own it and they don’t set the policy.

**in short:**
- Owner sets the rules 
- custodian maintains
- processor handles on the owner’s behalf

`ownership and all these roles are relative to the specific dataset. So a cloud provider is the processor for your data, but that very same provider is the data owner for its own information, like its customer list, its asset inventory, its billing records.
